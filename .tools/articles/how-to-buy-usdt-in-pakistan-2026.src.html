Step by step: buying USDT on Binance P2P

Binance is where most rupee-to-USDT volume happens, which matters because more sellers means tighter prices and faster releases. The whole flow takes about 10 minutes once your account is verified.

1

Create and verify your account

Sign up with referral code 775069017 to get a trading-fee discount on your account. Then complete identity verification with your CNIC. P2P is locked until verification passes, and it usually clears within minutes to a few hours.

2

Open P2P and set the filters

In the app go to Trade → P2P → Buy, choose USDT, set the fiat currency to PKR, and filter by the payment method you actually have, such as your bank, Raast, Easypaisa, SadaPay or NayaPay.

3

Pick a seller carefully

Look past the headline price. You want a high completion rate (above roughly 95%), hundreds of completed orders, a short average release time, and ideally a verified-merchant badge. A slightly worse rate from a proven seller beats a great rate from a new account.

4

Place the order and pay from your own account

Enter the PKR amount and Binance locks the seller's USDT in escrow. Send the exact amount to the account details shown on the order, and send it from an account in your own name that matches your Binance verification.

5

Mark as paid, then wait for release

Tap "Transferred, notify seller" only after the money has actually left your account. The seller confirms receipt and the USDT lands in your Funding wallet. If a seller doesn't release within the time limit, open an appeal from the order screen. Never settle it in a private chat.

How to avoid scams and frozen bank accounts

Almost every P2P horror story traces back to one of a handful of mistakes. Most freezes happen on the selling side: someone pays you with money taken from a scam victim, the victim complains to their bank, and your account is frozen while it's investigated. Buyers face less risk, but not zero.

👤

Own-name accounts only

Pay from, and receive into, accounts in your own name. Never accept or make third-party payments.

🔒

Stay inside the order

Escrow only protects trades completed on the platform. Anyone asking to move to WhatsApp is a scammer.

💬

Don't trust SMS screenshots

Confirm money has actually arrived in your banking app. Fake payment screenshots are the oldest trick going.

📋

Keep records

Screenshot each order and payment. If a dispute or bank query comes later, you'll need them.

Is buying USDT legal in Pakistan in 2026?

The Virtual Assets Act 2026 made the Pakistan Virtual Assets Regulatory Authority (PVARA) a permanent federal regulator. Licensing duties fall on platforms, not on individuals buying or holding crypto. Binance has held a PVARA No Objection Certificate since December 2025. That NOC is the first step toward a licence, not a completed licence, and as of our last check no exchange had announced a full PVARA licence.

In practice, holding and buying USDT sits inside a legal framework that is still being built. Gains and foreign income can create tax obligations, so keep your transaction history exported. If you're moving serious money, talk to a Pakistani tax practitioner.
