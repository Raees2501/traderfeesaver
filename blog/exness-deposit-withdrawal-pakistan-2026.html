The card trap: why your profit withdrawal fails

This catches a lot of new traders. You can deposit with a Visa or Mastercard, but in Pakistan Exness doesn't support profit withdrawals to cards. The card can only receive a refund of the amount you deposited with it, and everything above that has to go out through bank transfer or another available method.

On top of that, Exness's general withdrawal rules require you to withdraw through the same method you deposited with first, proportionally if you used several. So if you deposit by card and then try to send everything to a bank account, expect the system to route part of it back to the card first.

💡 Simplest setup

Deposit and withdraw through the same non-card method, bank transfer or crypto if available, from the start. One rail in, the same rail out, and you avoid the proportional-split headache.

Funding with USDT

Many Pakistani traders fund through USDT. They buy it with rupees on Binance P2P, then deposit it to Exness through the crypto option if their Personal Area shows one. Two rules matter:

  • Use your own wallet. Exness requires payment accounts to be in your name, so deposit from your own exchange account, not a friend's.

What about JazzCash and Easypaisa?

Dozens of websites state flatly that Exness accepts JazzCash and Easypaisa. We couldn't find either method documented on Exness's own public help centre, and the third-party claims contradict each other on fees and timing. Some Pakistani accounts may see local e-wallet options. Exness says payment-method availability depends on your registered region and verification status.

The only reliable check takes ten seconds: log in, open Personal Area → Deposit, and look at the list. If JazzCash or Easypaisa is there, it works for your account. If it isn't, no blog post changes that.

A note on regulation

Exness isn't licensed by Pakistan's SECP or the State Bank. Pakistani clients are onboarded through Exness's offshore entities, and moving money abroad for leveraged trading sits in a regulatory grey zone. Keep records of every deposit and withdrawal, and understand that the protections of a locally regulated broker don't apply.
