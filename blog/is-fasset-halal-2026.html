The honest answer

Fasset is built specifically as a Shariah-compliant platform, and it maintains formal Shariah advisory oversight rather than simply describing itself as halal in its marketing. That is a meaningful difference, and it puts Fasset in a much smaller group than the exchanges that add an "Islamic account" toggle.

But the question "is crypto halal?" is not settled. As of 2026, AAOIFI — the standard-setter most Islamic financial institutions defer to — has not issued a final Shariah standard on Bitcoin or cryptocurrency. No platform, Fasset included, can point to a universal certification, because none exists yet.

So the accurate framing is not "Fasset is halal, verified." It is: Fasset has structured its products around specific Shariah reasoning, disclosed who is doing that reasoning, and that reasoning is one that many contemporary scholars accept but not all.

Who actually oversees Fasset's compliance

The name to know is Mufti Faraz Adam, who has served as Fasset's Shariah Advisor for digital assets, tokenisation and Web3 since 2022, providing governance guidance on how its digital-asset products are structured.

This matters more than it might sound. In Islamic finance, the credibility of a "halal" label rests almost entirely on who signed off and what exactly they reviewed. A platform naming its advisor and their scope is giving you something checkable. A platform that only says "Shariah compliant" on a landing page is not.

Fasset has also moved further into regulated territory than most crypto platforms — it secured a provisional banking licence with the stated aim of operating as a stablecoin-powered Islamic bank, and runs Fasset Islamic as a regulated platform offering tokenised stocks and commodities.

The three questions people actually ask

1

Is the staking riba?

Fasset's position is that its staking rewards come from Proof-of-Stake validation — you are being rewarded for helping secure and validate a network, which is a service — rather than from lending money out at interest. Scholars who accept that distinction treat the reward as compensation for work, not riba. Scholars who view the staked asset itself as a loan to the protocol reach a different conclusion. This is the single biggest point of live disagreement.

2

Is intra-day trading gharar?

Fasset states that same-day trading is permissible on its platform because beneficial ownership transfers to you immediately on purchase. The Shariah objection to much conventional day trading is that the trader never owns the underlying asset — they hold a contract and settle a price difference. If ownership genuinely passes, the transaction is a sale of a thing you own, which changes the analysis. That said, this reasoning covers the structure of the trade, not the intention behind it — excessive speculation remains a separate concern regardless of settlement mechanics.

3

What about the underlying assets?

A compliant wrapper does not sanctify every asset inside it. Screening still matters: tokenised equities carry the same business-activity and debt-ratio screens as any Islamic equity investment, and not every token on any platform will pass every screen. If you are strict on this, check the specific assets you intend to hold rather than assuming platform-level compliance covers them.

How Fasset compares to an "Islamic account" on a normal exchange

Most mainstream exchanges and brokers offer a swap-free or Islamic account. That feature does one narrow thing: it removes overnight interest charges on leveraged positions. It says nothing about whether the assets, the contract structures, or the yield products are compliant.

What to ask your own scholar

If you want a ruling that actually applies to your situation, these are the specifics worth bringing rather than asking the general "is crypto halal" question:

  • Whether Proof-of-Stake validation rewards count as a service fee or as a return on a loan.
  • Whether immediate beneficial ownership satisfies the qabd (possession) requirement for a valid sale.
  • Whether tokenised equities inherit the Shariah screening of the underlying stock.
  • Whether holding USD-pegged stablecoins raises any currency-exchange (sarf) concerns in your school of thought.

Those four cover the substance of what Fasset does. An answer on them is worth far more than an answer about crypto in general.
